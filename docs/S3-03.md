# S3-03: Real-world testing and improvement of live album recognition

Owner: Emre. Reviewer: Oliver. Estimate: 5 SP. Branch:
`feature/S3-03-live-recognition-tuning`.

> Evaluate automatic detection, capture and recognition using real album
> covers. Test different lighting conditions, angles, distances and
> backgrounds. Identify recurring errors and improve detection, capture or
> recognition parameters based on the results.

Status on **2026-11-26**: two rounds done with recorded videos (the second
one against false outlines). Not yet checked on a phone with physical
records.

## Why

Two phone tests (covers shown on a laptop screen, 2026-11-16 and 11-25)
showed four problems:

- outlines on things that are not covers (keyboard keys, stickers, a laptop
  screen);
- the outline grabbing the laptop screen instead of the cover inside it, and
  morphing between the two;
- flicker: an outline vanished whenever one frame missed it;
- a wrong result (Rumours recognised as Blue Train), because the crop was
  taken while the outline still covered the whole screen.

## Test material and replay

There were no recordings of the app's camera feed with real records yet,
so four public short videos of people handling real records were used
(screen recordings, not committed):

| Clip | Scene | Frames at 5 fps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `held_bedroom` | ~20 sleeves held in both hands, swapped in the same spot, carpet and clothes behind | 200 |
| `held_text_overlay` | sleeves held below the face, bookshelf full of records behind, caption overlays | 148 |
| `record_store` | browsing a crate in a shop, very cluttered, sleeves pulled out and held close | 121 |
| `table_shrinkwrap` | sleeves in shrink-wrap held upright on a wooden table, glare, caption overlays | 413 |

`backend/scripts/prepare_replay_frames.py` turns videos into 5 fps grey
frames (the app analyses one frame every 200 ms), cuts off the phone's
status and navigation bars, and scales them so the analysis frame is
180 × 320 like the app's. `mobile/test/vision/replay_test.dart` runs every
frame through the real pipeline (`FrameAnalyzer`: detection, tracking,
best-frame capture) and writes metrics, the outlines per frame (JSON) and
the captured crops to `mobile/build/replay_out/<label>/`.

```bash
uv run backend/scripts/prepare_replay_frames.py clip1.mp4 clip2.mp4 --names a b --output mobile/build/replay
```

```bash
cd mobile && JAMSCAN_REPLAY_LABEL=after flutter test test/vision/replay_test.dart
```

The test skips itself when `build/replay` is missing.

Metrics, per clip and in total:

- **cover%**: frames with at least one outline on screen. Almost every
  frame of these clips shows a sleeve (except swaps and the shop clip), so
  higher is better.
- **short**: tracks shown for only one or two frames (flicker or false
  outlines).
- **reappear**: an outline vanishing and coming back with the same id
  (flicker).
- **nonsq%**: outlines not close to square (width/height outside
  0.75–1.33), ignoring covers cut off by the frame edge.
- **jitter**: median corner movement between consecutive detected frames,
  relative to the cover's size.
- **captures**: crops sent for recognition.

### Round 2: false outlines

The metrics above count outlines, not whether they are right. Rendering
the first round over the videos showed false outlines that the numbers
hid: a square over the person's face sat on screen in 70 % of the bedroom
clip. For round 2, every track of both runs was judged by eye from a
contact sheet of its frames and labelled real or false (labels carried
between runs by overlap, new tracks checked by hand). These are estimates:
a track is judged from a few of its frames.

| | frames with a real outline | frames with a false outline | false outline-frames |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Round 1 | 638 / 882 | 428 (49 %) | 477 |
| Round 2 | 638 / 882 | **286 (32 %)** | **325** |

Per clip, frames with a false outline: `held_bedroom` 141 → **3**,
`table_shrinkwrap` 139 → 136, `held_text_overlay` 134 → 131,
`record_store` 14 → 16. Before the review fixes below, round 2 stood at
272 (31 %): one fix (a corner merely near the frame edge no longer
counts) costs about 12 false frames here, and was kept because a sleeve
held close to the phone reaches the frame edge all the time.

What the false outlines are:

- **A square closed off by the sleeve's edge.** The person holds the
  sleeve below their face in front of a wardrobe or bookshelf. The
  wardrobe's or shelf's straight sides and the sleeve's own top edge close
  a second square, around the face. These are real straight lines, so edge
  support cannot reject them. Fixed when that square is cut off by the
  frame edge and the sleeve is not (the bedroom clip; see What changed).
- **Doorways and caption boxes.** In `table_shrinkwrap` the square around
  the face is a doorway behind the person, closed at the bottom by the
  video's black caption box. The caption overlays are part of the test
  videos, not of what the app's camera sees.
- **Shelves of spines** behind the person (`held_text_overlay`); the
  clearest ones are now rejected.
- **The sleeves themselves are rarely found in `held_text_overlay`**: they
  are held with both hands over the left and right sides and cut off by
  the bottom of the frame, so only one side is clear. Only 49 of its 148
  frames have a real outline (46 in round 1).

Round 2 metrics: cover% 85, 86 tracks, 8 short, 5 reappearances, nonsq 5 %,
jitter 0.023, 36 captures, 16.1 ms per frame (host). Of the 36 crops, 33
are clean sleeves (one is a text-only sleeve, Charli XCX's *Brat and it's
the same…*) and 3 are faces, all in the table clip (doorway and caption
box). The sleeve the face shape sits on is not seen in those frames, so
the new rule has nothing to compare with. Round 1 had 3 face crops in 35. Synthetic benchmark: unchanged, 75/84,
1.16 %, no false cover.

## What changed

**Detection** (`vision/cover_detector.dart`, new `vision/line_quads.dart`):

- Outlines are also built from straight lines: a Hough transform over thin
  edges, where each edge pixel votes only for lines along its own
  direction; pairs of near-parallel lines crossing at near-right angles
  give four-cornered candidates. Region tracing failed whenever a sleeve's
  edge touched other edges (hands, clothes, a crate); lines tolerate gaps
  where fingers cover a side. At most six lines per 10° direction band are
  kept, so planks, stripes or spines cannot crowd out a cover's sides.
- Every candidate, from regions or lines, is judged the same way:
  - each side must lie on edges running along it (one side may be mostly
    hidden by a hand);
  - covers are square, so width/height must be 0.69–1.45 (was 0.6–1.65) and
    squarer scores higher;
  - a sleeve's edge stops at its corners, so outlines whose lines run on
    past three or more corner ends (shelves, spines, seams) are rejected;
  - a side lying on the frame edge is not an edge: such a cover is judged
    on its other three sides, which must all be clear;
  - an outline close to a cover tracked in earlier frames gets up to 30 %
    extra, so it does not jump to a competing shape.
- Overlap rules: the best score wins, not the biggest outline (that rule
  picked the laptop screen around the cover). A candidate sharing more than
  a quarter of a better one's box is dropped.
- Round 2, **a shape built on a cover's edge**: when two outlines lie on
  either side of one shared side, the one with more sides along the frame
  edge is dropped, whichever of the two scored higher. In the app the user
  points the phone at the record, so the record is whole in the frame and
  shapes formed with the background reach its edges. Tracked covers count
  as the other side too, so the shape does not appear in frames where the
  sleeve itself is missed. When both are whole, both are kept: two
  tie-breaks were tried (which shape the shared edge stops at; which one
  fits better) and dropped, because the first was unreliable on pale
  sleeves against pale clothes and neither changed the results.
- Round 2, **shelves of spines**: the middle 75 % of an outline is
  resampled to a 48 × 48 grid, and an outline is rejected when at least
  80 % of the grid's columns (or rows) are crossed from side to side by
  straight lines. Shelves in the test videos score 0.78–0.96; the
  highest real cover in the videos scores 0.61 (lettering). The limit
  was lowered to 0.7 first, but a real cover in the synthetic benchmark
  (*The Velvet Underground & Nico*: a whip and a glossy boot, all long
  verticals) scores 0.70–0.75 and was lost. At 0.8 it only removes the
  clearest shelves (7 false outline-frames in these videos).

**Tracking** (`vision/cover_tracker.dart`):

- A cover is shown only after two detections, so single-frame shapes never
  flash up.
- A shown cover stays through up to two missed frames, drawn fainter.
- Small corner movements (noise) are smoothed strongly, large ones are
  followed at once.
- A detection much bigger or smaller than the tracked outline starts a new
  track instead of morphing the old one, and a confirmed cover replaces a
  lingering outline it overlaps.

**Capture** (`vision/best_frame_selector.dart`):

- Only outlines detected in the current frame are measured and captured.
- A capture needs the outline still for two steps and the artwork inside it
  unchanged (a 64-bit signature of the cover read from the analysis frame),
  so a sleeve being pulled out or slid in front is not captured.
- When the artwork inside a captured outline changes for two frames,
  someone swapped records in the same spot: the new sleeve is watched and
  captured like a new cover. The session (S2-11) still drops a crop of an
  album it already has.

**Overlay** (`widgets/live_camera_view.dart`): `AnimatedCoverOverlay`
moves outlines smoothly between analysed frames (5 per second) at screen
rate, fades new outlines in and lost ones out, and draws an outline kept
through a missed frame at 55 % opacity.

## Review

Gemini (Argon, high effort) reviewed the change. Fixed:

- Corners are paired by the closest rotation when outlines are smoothed,
  compared or animated. Near a 45° tilt the corner that counts as top-left
  can change between frames, which made the outline twist and reset the
  stillness count.
- The artwork signature is compared over all four rotations, for the same
  reason.
- Hough peaks are suppressed across the 0°/180° wrap, so a near-vertical
  side no longer gives two lines.
- Line pairs that cross inside the frame (bow-ties) are rejected, and all
  four line crossings must be near right angles.
- The selector drops its best view when the artwork changes, so it can no
  longer send the previous sleeve's frame and mark the new one as done.
- A hand or glare passing over a captured sleeve no longer counts as a
  swap; only steady new artwork does.
- Frames spent cut off by the frame edge no longer count towards the
  12-frame fallback, and a cover back after a gap must look steady again.
- Only shown covers act as priors and win ties in matching; a shape seen
  once cannot help itself along.
- The overlay's ticker only restarts when something moves, and is created
  in `initState`.
- The replay test handles odd frame sizes; the frame script never
  upscales, samples by timestamp and clears old frames.

Round 2 was reviewed by Gemini too. Fixed:

- An outline built on a cover's edge is now dropped even when it scored
  higher than the cover and was kept first (test: a hand hides half of
  the sleeve's side, so the door's square ranks above it).
- The three-cover limit is applied after the loop, so a lower-ranked
  cover can still displace a shape kept before it.
- Only sides along the frame edge count as cut off. A corner near the edge
  counted too, which would drop a whole sleeve held close to the phone in
  favour of a shape built on its edge. Cost measured above.
- Two sides count as shared only when they are close at both ends of
  their overlap, not merely at one point.
- The lettering test now has eight lines; Gemini's claim that the gaps
  between letters do not matter was checked and is wrong (letters pass at
  7–10 lines, solid bars fail from 7).

Not fixed, listed under limitations: a false tracked outline can still
block an edge-cut sleeve, and spines exactly as far apart as the grid
step can slip through the spine test.

Round 1, not changed: region candidates still pass the S2 checks before the common
scoring (line candidates cover the hand-held cases), Sobel gradients are
computed more than once per frame, and the painter always repaints (it is
animated anyway). Candidate counts stay bounded by 32 lines and 60 line
pairs; the cluttered shop clip runs at about 16 ms per frame on the host.

## Tests

`flutter analyze`: no issues. `flutter test`: 200 passed. New or changed:
hand-held sleeve, shelves of spines ignored, a near-vertical edge giving
one line, corner pairing across a change of top-left corner, tracker
confirmation, gaps, fast movement, size jumps and replacement, recapture
after a sleeve swap, no recapture when a hand passes over, overlay fade and
glide. Round 2: a shape closed off by a sleeve's edge (sleeve seen, and
sleeve only tracked, and a sleeve ranked below the shape that must still
replace it), a shelf compartment full of spines, and a cover with rows of
lettering still found. The seam, hand, shared-edge and spine tests
were checked to fail without their fixes. Tests that expected a cover on
the very first frame now expect it on the second.

## Known limitations and next steps

- **Faces above a sleeve, both whole.** When the square around the face is
  not cut off by the frame edge, or the sleeve is cut off at the bottom
  (face-centred videos), the shared-edge rule cannot tell which one is the
  cover and keeps both, or even favours the face (3 real-outline frames lost
  in `held_text_overlay`). Telling a face or doorway from a cover needs
  appearance, not geometry: a small learned cover/not-cover check on the
  crop is the next step if phone footage shows this. Meanwhile the
  backend's match threshold should reject such crops (3 of 36) as no match.
- **A false outline the tracker holds.** The shared-edge rule also uses
  tracked outlines. If a false shape is tracked while the real sleeve is
  missed, and the sleeve then appears cut off by the frame edge, the sleeve
  is dropped in favour of the tracked shape until that track ends.
- **Spines at the grid's spacing.** The spine test samples a 48 × 48 grid;
  spines spaced at a multiple of the grid step can fall between samples
  and pass (seen with a synthetic shelf of spines 6 px apart).
- **Striped covers.** A cover made of long straight stripes across most of
  its width, such as a field of thin vertical lines, would be rejected
  like a shelf.
- **Swaps in one spot.** Two records swapped quickly in the same spot can
  be captured once between them when the outline jumps during the swap
  (two Lana Del Rey albums in the table clip: each round captured one).
- **Shop crate.** Sleeves pulled out of a crate are often too close (bigger
  than the frame) or moving, so little is captured. That is the right
  call; the "move back" and "hold steady" hints cover it.
- **Recognition voting** (sending 2–3 crops per sleeve and agreeing on the
  result before showing it) is not done yet. It belongs with S3-04's
  threshold calibration.
- **Next**: record the app's own camera stream on an Android phone with
  physical records (light, angle, distance, background as in S3-03), add
  those clips to the replay, and compare.
